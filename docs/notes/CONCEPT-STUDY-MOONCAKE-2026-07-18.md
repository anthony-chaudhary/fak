## Worldview
Mooncake is Kimi/Moonshot's serving substrate (FAST'25 Best Paper). It pools idle CPU/DRAM/SSD across a GPU cluster into a **shared, tiered, distributed KV-cache object store**, so a *separated* prefill cluster and decode cluster reuse KV globally over a topology-aware multi-NIC RDMA fabric — optimizing **cluster goodput under SLO** where a rank/NIC/peer is broken at all times (75% more requests under SLO). Defaults reveal priority: eviction watermark-driven and on; everything expensive opt-in; **nothing** blocks the put/get/transfer hot path or deadlocks on a dead peer (best-effort under-replication over failure, near-LRU via `nth_element`, timeout-flips-membership, availability over strict consistency). Three pillars: **Transfer Engine** (topology NIC ranking + retry-indexed failover + rail circuit-breakers; `tent/` = a QoS/deadline-aware rewrite), **Store** (master placement, two-band near-LRU eviction, soft/hard pin, CMSketch-gated promotion, reserve/commit quota, fork-COW+oplog HA), **EP/PG** (fault-tolerant elastic expert parallelism via an `active_ranks` mask).

**The vs-fak thesis**: fak owns the **observability + identity** halves strongly — distinct CacheFill/Evict/Revoke events, self-instrumenting `internal/tracesink`, gap-detecting `pkg/fakclient/changes_consumer.go`, fencing tokens `internal/scmbridge/fence.go`, COW `PagedKV.Fork()`, capability matrix `internal/model/kv_transport_registry.go`, GDSF value-density eviction `internal/cacheprice/retention.go` — but stops short of the **closed-loop CONTROL** halves Mooncake pushes into (banded eviction into pinned victims, sketch-gated re-admission, proportional fairness, EDF+predicted-miss shedding, receiver-credit flow control, fault-tolerant partial-combine). The four filed leaves are the cleanest net-new control borrows.
